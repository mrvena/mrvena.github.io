Today's Learning Objective

I can write and graph linear equations using point-slope form when given a specific point and a slope, or when given two points on a line.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Tech Support Fees

Example: A computer technician charges an hourly rate (slope) plus a service fee. If you know one total bill and the hours worked, you can write an equation for any future bills!

Taxi & Rideshares

Example: A rideshare app charges per mile. If you know the cost of a 2-mile trip and the rate per mile, you can easily map out the entire pricing structure.

Subscription Plans

Example: A streaming service charges a setup fee and a monthly cost. Knowing two different friends' total costs allows you to write the exact pricing equation.

Pacing & Distance

Example: A runner keeps a steady pace. If you record their distance at minute 10 and minute 20, you can calculate their speed and track the whole race.

Key Vocabulary Terms

point-slope form: The equation of a nonvertical line with slope m and through point (x₁, y₁) written as y - y₁ = m(x - x₁).
slope (m): The constant rate of change of a line, representing how steep the line is.
coordinate point: An ordered pair (x, y) representing a specific location on the coordinate plane.
slope formula: The ratio of the change in y to the change in x between two points: m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁).
linear equation: An equation whose graph forms a straight line.
cross-products rule: A method used to verify the relationship between the slope formula and point-slope form.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Point-Slope Form from a Point & Slope

Core Formula: y - y₁ = m(x - x₁)
Example Problem Statement:

Write the point-slope form of a line with a slope of 7 that passes through the point (-3, 9).

Step 1: Identify Given Values

Problem: Write the equation using slope 7 and point (-3, 9).

The slope (m) is 7.
The coordinate point (x₁, y₁) is (-3, 9).

Step 2: Substitute into the Formula

Start with: y - y₁ = m(x - x₁)
Substitute: y - 9 = 7(x - (-3))
Simplify the double negative: y - 9 = 7(x + 3)

Equation Builder
y - y₁ = m(x - x₁)
y - 9 = 7[x - (-3)]

y - 9 = 7(x + 3)

Error to Avoid: Mixing Up Signs!

Always remember the formula uses subtraction naturally: (x - x₁). If you substitute a negative number, like x₁ = -3, subtracting a negative becomes positive addition! Be very careful with your signs.

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Point-Slope Form Given Two Points

Slope Formula: m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)
Example Problem Statement:

Write an equation in point-slope form for a line passing through the points (4, 11) and (8, 23).

Step 1: Find the Slope Using Both Points

Problem: (4, 11) and (8, 23)

m = (23 - 11) / (8 - 4)
m = 12 / 4 = 3. The slope is 3.

Step 2: Choose ONE Point & Substitute

You can choose either point to create a valid equation!
• If using (4, 11): y - 11 = 3(x - 4)
• If using (8, 23): y - 23 = 3(x - 8)
Both are 100% correct and describe the exact same line!

Two Paths to Success
m = 3
Using (4, 11)

y - 11 = 3(x - 4)
Using (8, 23)

y - 23 = 3(x - 8)

Error to Avoid: Flipping the Numerator and Denominator!

When finding the slope, make sure you subtract the y-values in the numerator (top) and the x-values in the denominator (bottom). Don't calculate change in x over change in y!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. The Structure

The point-slope form is consistently written as y - y₁ = m(x - x₁). It requires only one known point and the slope.

2. Watch Signs

Since the formula subtracts the coordinates natively, a negative coordinate will turn the expression into positive addition!

3. Using Two Points

When given two points, always calculate the slope first using m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁), then substitute one point.

4. Multiple Answers

If a line passes through multiple specific points, writing the equation with any of those points is completely correct!
