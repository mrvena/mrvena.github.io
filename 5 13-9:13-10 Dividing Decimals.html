Today's Learning Objective

I can use models and math rules to divide a decimal by another decimal.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Working with Money

When you need to figure out how many $0.25 quarters fit into $3.75, you are dividing a decimal by a decimal!

Food Portions

A chef splitting 4.5 pounds of cheese into equal 0.5-pound servings relies on dividing decimals to get exact portions.

Crafting & Building

Cutting a long 0.85-meter ribbon into multiple smaller 0.17-meter pieces for art projects uses decimal division.

Speed & Distance

If a runner completes a race in 12.6 minutes and each lap takes 1.4 minutes, you can divide to find out how many laps they ran.

Key Vocabulary Terms

dividend: The amount that you want to divide up into smaller groups.
divisor: The number you divide by. It tells you the size of the groups.
quotient: The final answer you get after you divide the numbers.
tenths: The first place value to the right of the decimal point (0.1).
hundredths: The second place value to the right of the decimal point (0.01).
base-ten model: Visual blocks or grids used to represent decimals and groups visually.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Model Dividing a Decimal by a Decimal

Example Topic: Using Grids
Example Problem Statement:

Find 0.60 ÷ 0.12. Use a model to divide.

Step 1: Model the Dividend

Problem: Find 0.60 ÷ 0.12

First, model the dividend, which is 0.60. On a hundredths grid, you would shade in 60 hundredths (or 60 small blocks).

Step 2: Find Groups of the Divisor

Problem: Find 0.60 ÷ 0.12

Next, identify the divisor, which is 0.12 (12 hundredths). Count how many groups of 12 hundredths you can make out of the 60 hundredths you shaded.

You can make exactly 5 groups of 12 hundredths. So, 0.60 ÷ 0.12 = 5.

Model Logic
Total blocks shaded (Dividend)
60 blocks
Blocks in each group (Divisor)
12 blocks per group
Total number of groups (Quotient)
5

Error to Avoid: Counting Individual Blocks!

When you model division, your quotient is the number of groups you made, NOT the total number of blocks you counted! Always ask yourself: "How many full groups did I circle?"

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Dividing by Moving the Decimal Point

Example Topic: Math Rules
Example Problem Statement:

Divide 3.68 by 0.4 using algebraic math rules.

Step 1: Move the Decimal in the Divisor

Problem: Divide 3.68 by 0.4

Move the decimal point of the divisor (0.4) enough places to form a whole number. Since 0.4 has one digit after the decimal, you move it 1 place to the right to make it a 4.

Step 2: Move the Decimal in the Dividend

Problem: Divide 3.68 by 0.4

Move the decimal point in the dividend (3.68) to the right the same number of places. Moving it 1 place turns 3.68 into 36.8.

Now place the decimal point in your quotient directly above where it sits in the dividend, and divide normally: 36.8 ÷ 4 = 9.2.

Moving Decimals
3.68 ÷ 0.4

(Move points 1 space to the right)

36.8 ÷ 4
Final Quotient: 9.2

Error to Avoid: Moving Decimals Unequally!

A common mistake is moving the decimal point in the dividend a different number of places than in the divisor. If you move the divisor 2 spaces, you must move the dividend 2 spaces!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Using Models

A hundredths grid is a great tool. Find how many groups of the divisor fit inside the total dividend!

2. Moving Decimals

To divide by a decimal, always move the decimal point to the right until the divisor is a whole number.

3. Keep it Equal

Whatever you do to the divisor, you must do to the dividend. Move the decimal point the same number of spaces for both.

4. Check Your Work

You can always check your division by multiplying your quotient by your original divisor to see if you get the dividend.
