Today's Learning Objective

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Travel & Fuel

If a car uses a certain amount of gas to drive a specific distance, proportions let us calculate exactly how much fuel is needed for a longer road trip.

Baking & Recipes

When you have a recipe that makes a dozen cookies but you need to make three dozen, proportions keep your ingredients perfectly balanced.

Pay & Hours Worked

If you know how much money you earn for working a few hours, a direct proportion helps you find out exactly how much you will make in a whole week.

Science & Physics

In physics, the amount a spring stretches depends directly on the weight attached to it. This constant relationship is a direct proportion.

Key Vocabulary Terms

proportion: An equation stating that two ratios or fractions are equivalent.
means: The middle terms of a proportion when written in ratio format (b and c in a:b = c:d).
extremes: The end terms of a proportion when written in ratio format (a and d in a:b = c:d).
cross-products rule: In a true proportion, the product of the means equals the product of the extremes (ad = bc).
direct proportion: A relationship where an increase or decrease in one quantity causes the same kind of change in another.
like units: Setting up a proportion so the numerators share the same unit, and denominators share the same unit.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Solving Proportions

Cross Products Rule: a × d = b × c
Example Problem Statement:

Find the missing term in the proportion: n / 52 = 68 / 136.

Step 1: Set up the Cross Products

Problem: Solve for n in n / 52 = 68 / 136.

Multiply the numerator of the first fraction by the denominator of the second fraction (n × 136). Then, multiply the denominator of the first fraction by the numerator of the second fraction (52 × 68). Set them equal to each other.

Step 2: Multiply and Solve

136n = 3536

To isolate the variable n, divide both sides by 136.
3536 ÷ 136 = 26. The missing term is 26.

Error to Avoid: Multiplying Numerators Together!

Never multiply the top number by the top number. The cross-products rule requires you to multiply diagonally (numerator times opposite denominator).

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Direct Proportions

Unlike Units: dollars / hours = dollars / hours
Example Problem Statement:

A graphic designer earns $812 for working 28 hours. At that rate, how much money will the designer earn for working 32 hours?

Step 1: Set Up the Direct Proportion

Problem: Earns $812 in 28 hours. How much in 32 hours?

Set up ratios mapping dollars to hours. Let m equal the missing money. Ensure the units match in both fractions: 812 / 28 = m / 32.

Step 2: Cross Multiply and Solve

• Cross multiply: 28m = 812 × 32
• Multiply: 812 × 32 = 25984
• Divide by 28: 25984 ÷ 28 = 928
The designer will earn $928 for working 32 hours.

Error to Avoid: Mixing Up Units!

Always make sure you line up your units. If you put "dollars" on the top of the left fraction, "dollars" must also be on the top of the right fraction!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Proportions

A proportion simply states that two ratios are entirely equivalent, just like equivalent fractions.

2. Cross-Products

Multiply the numerator of one ratio by the denominator of the other to solve for the missing piece.

3. Direct Proportions

When one amount goes up, the other amount goes up at a constant matching rate!

4. Match Units

When setting up a word problem, make sure you put the same units in the numerators and the same units in the denominators.
