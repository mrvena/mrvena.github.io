Today's Learning Objective

I can solve division problems with remainders, and I can use divisibility rules to find out if numbers can be divided evenly!

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Baking Treats

If you bake 25 cookies and share them with 3 friends, division helps you find out how many each person gets, and how many are left over!

Packing Boxes

If you need to fit 60 books onto shelves and each shelf holds 8 books, division tells you exactly how many shelves you can fill.

Planting Gardens

When you want exactly the same number of flower plants in every row, divisibility rules help you choose the right number of rows.

Making Teams

If you have a large class and want to know if you can make equal teams of 5, the divisibility rule for 5 makes it a quick check!

Key Vocabulary Terms

divide: To split a number into equal groups.
quotient: The answer to a division problem (how many are in each group).
remainder: The amount left over that cannot be split evenly into the groups.
divisible: When a number can be divided evenly by another number, with no remainder!
divisibility rules: Quick patterns or tricks to see if a number is divisible by another number.
multiplication fact: Basic facts like 5 × 7 = 35 that help you estimate and solve division.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: One-Digit Quotients & Remainders

Example Operation: 37 ÷ 5
Example Problem Statement:

Find the quotient and the remainder when 37 is divided by 5.

Step 1: Estimate Using Multiplication

Problem: Find 37 ÷ 5.

Think of a basic multiplication fact with 5 that gets you close to 37 without going over.
5 × 7 = 35 (less than 37)
5 × 8 = 40 (greater than 37)
So, we will try 7.

Step 2: Subtract to Find the Remainder

Problem: We have 7 groups of 5.

Subtract the 35 from our original 37 to see what is left over.
37 - 35 = 2.
The quotient is 7, and the remainder is 2!

Division Setup

5 ) 37
- 35
2 Remainder

Error to Avoid: A Remainder That Is Too Big!

The remainder must always be less than the divisor. If your remainder is bigger than the number you are dividing by, it means you can make another group!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Divisibility Rules

Example Number: 135
Example Problem Statement:

Tell whether the number 135 is divisible by 3, 5, or 10 using divisibility rules.

Rule 1: Check the Ones Digit (5 and 10)

Problem: Is 135 divisible by 5 or 10?

• The ones digit in 135 is 5.
• A number is divisible by 5 if the ones digit is 0 or 5. So, 135 is divisible by 5.
• A number is divisible by 10 only if the ones digit is 0. So, 135 is not divisible by 10.

Rule 2: Add the Digits (3)

Problem: Is 135 divisible by 3?

• Add all the digits together: 1 + 3 + 5 = 9.
• Check if the sum is divisible by 3. Since 9 ÷ 3 = 3 with no remainder, 135 is divisible by 3!

Divisibility Check for 135
1 3 5

Ends in 5 → Divisible by 5

Does not end in 0 → Not divisible by 10


1 + 3 + 5 = 9
9 is divisible by 3, so 135 is divisible by 3!

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to Add the Digits for 3!

Don't just look at the last digit for the rule of 3. You must add all the digits together first. Just because a number is odd doesn't mean it's divisible by 3!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Divide Safely

When you divide, use multiplication facts to estimate the quotient. Don't go over the original number!

2. Remainders

The remainder is what's left over. Remember, your remainder must always be smaller than the number you divide by.

3. Look at the Ends

Numbers ending in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 are divisible by 2. Ends in 0 or 5? Divisible by 5. Ends in 0? Divisible by 10.

4. Rule of 3

To see if a number is divisible by 3, add all of its digits together first, then see if the sum works!
