Today's Learning Objective

I can use number lines to understand positive and negative integers, find their opposites, and use them to represent real-world situations like temperature, money, and elevation.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Weather & Temperature

Example: A severe freeze drops the temperature to 4 degrees below zero. We use negative integers like -4 to measure how cold it gets!

Banking & Money

Example: If you deposit money, your balance goes up (positive). If you write a check or owe someone, your balance goes down (negative).

Elevation & Depth

Example: A mountain peak is high above sea level (positive). A diver swimming below the surface is represented by a negative depth.

Sports & Games

Example: In football, gaining yards moves you forward (+), but getting tackled behind the line of scrimmage results in losing yards (-).

Key Vocabulary Terms

integers: The set of whole numbers and their opposites. They can be positive, negative, or zero.
positive integers: Integers that are greater than zero. They appear to the right of zero on a horizontal number line.
negative integers: Integers that are less than zero. They appear to the left of zero on a horizontal number line.
zero: The number zero is right in the middle of the number line. It is neither positive nor negative.
opposites: Two integers that are the exact same distance from zero, but on opposite sides of the number line.
vertical number line: A number line drawn up and down, often used for temperature (thermometer) or elevation (above/below sea level).
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Integers and Opposites

Example Problem Statement:

Find the opposite of the integer -8.

Step 1: Locate the Number on the Number Line

Problem: Find the opposite of -8.

First, find where -8 lives on the number line. Since it is a negative number, it is located exactly 8 spaces to the left of zero.

Step 2: Find the Opposite Distance

Problem: Find the opposite of -8.

Opposites are the same distance from zero, just on the other side. Since -8 is 8 spaces to the left, its opposite must be 8 spaces to the right of zero.
The number 8 spaces to the right of zero is positive 8.

Finding Opposites
-8 0 8
The opposite of -8 is 8

Rule to Remember: The Opposite of an Opposite!

Every integer has an opposite. The opposite of the opposite of a number is the original number! For example, the opposite of the opposite of 3 is just 3.

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Real-World Integers & Vertical Lines

Example Problem Statement:

A scuba diver is exploring a reef 25 feet below sea level. Write the integer that represents this situation.

Step 1: Identify Zero in the Real World Context

Problem: A diver is 25 feet below sea level.

In this situation, "sea level" acts as zero on a vertical number line. Anything above the water is positive, and anything below the water is negative.

Step 2: Assign Positive or Negative

• Because the diver is below sea level, we use a negative integer.
• We take the number 25 and make it negative: -25.
The final integer representing the diver's depth is -25.

Vertical Number Line
0 (Sea Level)
Location:
25 ft Below Zero

Integer:
-25

Error to Avoid: Forgetting the Meaning of Zero!

Always figure out what zero means in your story. Zero could mean sea level, ground level, freezing point of water, or having an empty bank account!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. The Integer Family

Integers are all the whole numbers and their opposites. They include positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.

2. The Middle Ground

Zero is right in the middle of the number line. It is perfectly neutral—neither positive nor negative!

3. Opposites

Two numbers that are the same exact distance from zero are called opposites. (Example: 4 and -4). The opposite of an opposite is the original number!

4. Real World Clues

Words like "deposit", "above", and "gain" usually mean positive integers. Words like "withdrawal", "below", and "loss" mean negative integers.
