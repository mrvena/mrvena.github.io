Today's Learning Objective

I can use division rules to find quotients and I can use arrays to understand how multiplication and division are related.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Sharing Fairly

Example: If you have stickers and want to give them to your friends, division helps you make sure everyone gets the exact same amount!

Setting up Rooms

Example: When teachers arrange desks into equal rows, they use arrays. Multiplication and division help them know exactly how many desks fit.

Sports Teams

Example: If 30 kids show up to play basketball, dividing them into 5 equal teams ensures the game is fair for everyone.

Empty Boxes

Example: If you have 0 toys to share among 4 friends, division rules tell you quickly that everyone gets 0 toys.

Key Vocabulary Terms

divide: To separate a set into equal parts, or to share a set equally.
set: A group of objects or numbers.
dividend: The total number that is being divided.
divisor: The number you are dividing by.
quotient: The answer you get after dividing.
array: An arrangement of objects into equal rows and columns.
multiplication fact: A math sentence showing two numbers multiplied together.
Section 2 • Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Division Rules

Example Problem 1:

Find the quotient of 17 divided by 1.

Rule 1: Dividing by 1

Problem: Find the quotient of 17 divided by 1.

When the divisor is 1, the quotient is the same as the dividend.

Answer: 17 ÷ 1 = 17

Example Problem 2:

Find the quotient of 19 divided by 19.

Rule 2: Dividing by itself

Problem: Find the quotient of 19 divided by 19.

When the divisor and the dividend are the same nonzero number, the quotient is always 1.

Answer: 19 ÷ 19 = 1

Example Problem 3:

Find the quotient of 0 divided by 4.

Rule 3: Zero dividend

Problem: Find the quotient of 0 divided by 4.

When the dividend is 0, the quotient is 0.

Answer: 0 ÷ 4 = 0

Section 4 • Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Relate Multiplication and Division

Example Problem 4:

There are 15 students that are put in groups of 3. How many groups of students are there? Use a related multiplication fact to find the quotient.

Step 1: Write the division problem

Problem: There are 15 students that are put in groups of 3. How many groups are there?

We want to find: 15 ÷ 3 = ?

Step 2: "Undo" with Multiplication

Multiplication and division are related. Multiplication "undoes" division.
• Instead of 15 ÷ 3 = ?, think: ? × 3 = 15
• We know that 5 × 3 = 15.
• So, 15 ÷ 3 = 5.
There are 5 groups of students!

Fact Family
5 × 3 = 15

15 ÷ 3 = 5
Multiplying by 3 "undoes" dividing by 3!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

Rule of 1

When you divide any number by 1, the quotient is just that exact same number.

Same Number

When you divide a non-zero number by itself, the quotient is always exactly 1!

Rule of 0

When the dividend (starting number) is 0, the quotient is always 0.

Undoing

Multiplication undoes division! You can find any quotient by thinking of the related multiplication fact.
