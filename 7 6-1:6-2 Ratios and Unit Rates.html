Today's Learning Objective

I can write ratios in different forms, find equivalent ratios in simplest form, calculate unit rates, and figure out the best buy when shopping.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Cooking & Recipes

Example: A recipe calls for 2 cups of flour to 1 cup of sugar. We use equivalent ratios to safely double or triple the recipe without ruining the food!

Finding the Best Deal

Example: Should you buy a 3-pack of shirts or a 5-pack? Calculating the unit cost (price for just one shirt) tells you which option saves you money.

Travel & Distance

Example: When planning a road trip, using the unit rate of "miles per gallon" helps you figure out exactly how much fuel you'll need to reach your destination.

Sports Statistics

Example: Comparing the distance between bases in baseball versus softball uses a ratio. Win-loss records in sports are also simple ratios!

Key Vocabulary Terms

ratio: A comparison of two quantities, a and b, by division.
terms of the ratio: The specific quantities being compared in the ratio.
equivalent ratios: Ratios that have the same value. Found by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number.
simplest form: A ratio where the greatest common factor (GCF) of the terms is 1.
rate: A special type of ratio that compares two unlike quantities (e.g., miles and hours).
unit rate / unit cost: A rate simplified to have a denominator of 1 unit. Unit cost is the price for one item.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Ratios and Equivalent Ratios

Example Ratio: 16 to 24
Example Problem Statement:

A parking lot has 16 cars and 24 trucks. Write the ratio of cars to trucks in three different forms, and then find the ratio in simplest form.

Step 1: Write the Three Forms

Problem: Write the ratio of cars (16) to trucks (24).

• Word Form: 16 to 24
• Ratio Form: 16:24
• Fraction Form: 16/24

Step 2: Simplify the Ratio

Problem: Find the simplest form of 16/24.

To simplify, find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) of 16 and 24. The biggest number that divides evenly into both is 8.
• 16 ÷ 8 = 2
• 24 ÷ 8 = 3
The simplest form is 2 to 3, 2:3, or 2/3.

Error to Avoid: Mixing Up the Order!

The order of the words in the problem must match the order of your numbers! If it asks for cars to trucks, you must put the cars number first (or on top). If you write trucks to cars, your ratio is incorrect!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Rates, Unit Rates, and Unit Cost

Example Rates: 4 for $68 vs 6 for $96
Example Problem Statement:

Store A sells 4 shirts for $68. Store B sells 6 shirts for $96. Find the unit cost for each to determine which store is the better buy.

Step 1: Find Store A Unit Cost

Problem: Find the cost of 1 shirt at Store A.

Set up the rate: Cost divided by Number of Shirts.
68 ÷ 4 = 17.
The unit cost at Store A is $17 per shirt.

Step 2: Find Store B Unit Cost & Compare

Problem: Find the cost of 1 shirt at Store B.

Set up the rate: Cost divided by Number of Shirts.
96 ÷ 6 = 16.
The unit cost at Store B is $16 per shirt.
Since $16 is less than $17, Store B is the better buy.

Unit Cost Comparison
Store A
$68 ÷ 4

$17
Store B
$96 ÷ 6

$16
Store B is cheaper!

Error to Avoid: Dividing Backwards!

When trying to find the unit cost (price per item), you must always divide the total money by the number of items. Never divide the items by the money!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Ratio Forms

You can write a ratio with words (a to b), with a colon (a:b), or as a fraction (a/b).

2. Simplest Form

Always find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) and divide both terms by it to find the simplest form.

3. Unit Rate

A rate is just a ratio comparing unlike quantities. A unit rate simplifies the bottom number to exactly 1.

4. Best Buy

Divide the price by the amount to get the unit cost. The item with the lowest unit cost is the better buy!
