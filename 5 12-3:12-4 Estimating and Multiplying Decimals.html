Today's Learning Objective

I can estimate the products of decimals, and I can multiply decimals by whole numbers to find the exact answer.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Fuel & Distance

Example: A car gets 32.1 miles per gallon and the tank holds 12.4 gallons. You estimate the product to quickly know how far you can drive!

Buying by the Pound

Example: A deli uses 0.25 pounds of meat for each sandwich. If you need 23 sandwiches, you multiply a decimal by a whole number to buy enough meat.

Space Exploration

Example: Planets are measured in Astronomical Units (AU). Jupiter is about 13.4 times as far from the sun as Mercury. Multiplying finds the exact distance.

Nutrition

Example: One banana has 0.5 mg of Vitamin B6. If you eat 4 bananas, you need to multiply 0.5 by 4 to track your vitamins.

Key Vocabulary Terms

estimate: To find a number close to an exact amount to check if an answer is reasonable.
front-end estimation: Keeping the nonzero front digit of a number and changing the rest to zeros before multiplying.
rounding: Adjusting a number to its greatest place value to make mental math easier (e.g., 3.8 becomes 4).
decimal: A number with one or more digits to the right of the decimal point.
whole number: A counting number from zero to infinity, without fractions or decimals.
product: The final answer you get when you multiply two or more numbers together.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Estimating Decimal Products

Example Problem: 3.8 × 12
Example Problem Statement:

A runner jogs 3.8 miles every day. If they run for 12 days, about how many miles did they run in total? Estimate the product using rounding.

Step 1: Round Each Factor to the Greatest Place

Problem: Estimate the product of 3.8 and 12 using rounding.

Look at 3.8. The greatest place is the ones place. 3.8 rounds up to 4.
Look at 12. The greatest place is the tens place. 12 rounds down to 10.

Step 2: Multiply the Rounded Numbers

Problem: Multiply the newly rounded numbers to find the estimate.

Now multiply: 4 × 10 = 40.
The runner jogged about 40 miles in total!

Estimated Product

Error to Avoid: Forgetting the Difference Between Rounding and Front-End!

With rounding, you look at the next digit to see if you round up or stay the same. With front-end estimation, you ONLY keep the very first number and turn everything else to zeros (e.g., 4.8 becomes 4, not 5).

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Multiplying Decimals by Whole Numbers

Example Problem: 4 × 1.25
Example Problem Statement:

You buy 4 bags of apples. Each bag weighs 1.25 pounds. Exactly how many pounds of apples did you buy?

Step 1: Multiply Like Whole Numbers

Problem: Multiply 4 × 1.25.

Ignore the decimal point at first. Multiply the numbers as if they were both whole numbers: 4 × 125 = 500.

Step 2: Place the Decimal Point

• Count the total number of decimal places in your original factors.
• The number 4 has 0 decimal places.
• The number 1.25 has 2 decimal places (the .25).
• Start at the far right of 500, and move the decimal point 2 places to the left.
The final exact product is 5.00 (or just 5).

(Move decimal 2 spots left)
Final Exact Product: 5.00

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Check Yourself

Estimating your product first helps you know if your exact answer makes sense!

2. Front-End

Keep only the first nonzero digit of each factor, turn the rest to zeros, and multiply!

3. Rounding

Round numbers to their greatest place value to make calculating estimates easy in your head.

4. Exact Placements

Multiply like whole numbers. Then count total decimal places in the factors to place the decimal point in the product.
