Today's Learning Objective

I can solve multistep math problems using multiplication and division from left to right, and I can check my work by going backward!

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Field Trips

Example: If a class takes multiple buses and then needs to split into smaller groups at a zoo, we use multiplication and division to find the group sizes.

Baking & Cooking

Example: A bakery makes several trays of cookies and then divides them evenly into gift boxes for customers to buy.

Sports & Time

Example: Converting hours into minutes to find out exactly how long you should spend at different exercise stations.

Sharing Supplies

Example: Finding out how many sheets of paper or art supplies go into different classroom folders.

Key Vocabulary Terms

order of operations: The rules that tell us which math step to do first to get the correct answer.
left to right: The direction we move when solving an equation that has both multiplication and division.
equation: A math sentence that uses an equal sign to show that two amounts are exactly the same.
expression: A math phrase with numbers and symbols, but no equal sign.
unknown quantity: A missing number we need to find, which is often represented by a letter in an equation.
working backward: A strategy to check your answer by doing the opposite math operations in reverse order.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Solving Left to Right

Example Equation: 2 × 39 ÷ 6 = g
Example Problem Statement:

A teacher buys 2 boxes of erasers. Each box has 39 erasers. The teacher divides them equally among 6 groups of students. How many erasers does each group get?

Step 1: Multiply to find the total

Problem: A teacher buys 2 boxes of erasers. Each box has 39 erasers...

Following the order of operations from left to right, we multiply first.
2 × 39 = 78 total erasers.

Step 2: Divide by the number of groups

Problem: ...The teacher divides them equally among 6 groups of students.

Now take the total and divide it by 6 groups.
78 ÷ 6 = 13. Each group gets 13 erasers.

Solving & Checking Work
Solve Left to Right
2 × 39 ÷ 6
78 ÷ 6
13
Work Backward to Check
13 × 6 = 78
78 ÷ 2 = 39
It matches!

Error to Avoid: Doing operations out of order!

When you see multiplication and division in the same equation, do not skip around. You must solve them in order starting from the left and moving to the right.

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Real World Multistep & Time

Remember: 1 Hour = 60 Minutes
Example Problem Statement:

A painter works for 4 hours. They spend the exact same amount of minutes painting 15 different small canvases. How many minutes do they spend on each canvas?

Step 1: Convert to the right unit

Problem: A painter works for 4 hours...

The question asks for the answer in minutes. We must convert 4 hours into minutes first. There are 60 minutes in an hour.
4 × 60 = 240 minutes.

Step 2: Divide to find the share

Problem: ...They spend the exact same amount of minutes painting 15 different small canvases.

Divide the total minutes evenly among the 15 canvases.
240 ÷ 15 = 16. They spend 16 minutes on each canvas!

Time Conversion Machine
4 Hours

4 × 60 minutes

240 minutes

240 ÷ 15 canvases
Final Minutes: 16

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to convert units!

You cannot just divide the hours directly if the final answer needs to be in minutes. Always multiply by 60 first!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Left to Right

Whenever multiplication and division are the only steps left, always start from the left side and work your way to the right side!

2. Checking Work

You can check your answers by working backward. Start with your answer and do the opposite math operations.

3. Converting Units

If a problem gives you hours but asks for minutes, multiply by 60 before doing anything else!

4. Unknown Quantities

Sometimes the answer we need to find is represented by a letter in an equation, like "g".
