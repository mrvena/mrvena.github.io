Today's Learning Objective

I can use part-to-whole ratios to solve proportion problems, and I can use map scales and scale factors to find the actual dimensions of scale drawings and models.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Mixing Recipes

Example: Creating a smoothie recipe that needs exact ratios of strawberries to bananas so it tastes perfect no matter how large the batch is.

Voting & Elections

Example: Seeing early voting trends like "2 votes for Candidate A for every 1 vote for Candidate B" helps predict the final total.

Maps & Navigation

Example: Using a map scale where 1 inch equals 50 miles lets you figure out the real driving distance across a state.

Architecture Models

Example: Building a scale model of a skyscraper where 1 foot on the model perfectly represents 100 feet in real life.

Key Vocabulary Terms

proportion: An equation stating that two ratios are equal to each other.
part-to-whole ratio: A ratio that compares a single part of a group to the total sum of all parts in the group.
scale drawing: A two-dimensional drawing that is proportional in size to an actual object.
map scale: The ratio of the distance on a map to the actual real-world distance.
scale model: A three-dimensional representation that accurately preserves proportional sizes of a real object.
scale factor: A scale written as a ratio in simplest form comparing the scale model to the actual object.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Proportions by Parts

Key Strategy: Find the Whole First!
Example Problem Statement:

A recipe for fruit punch uses 4 cups of pineapple juice and 3 cups of orange juice. If you want to make a large batch totaling 35 cups of punch, how many cups of pineapple juice will you need?

Step 1: Find the Total Parts (The Whole)

Problem: Find the total parts in the punch.

Add the pineapple parts to the orange parts to find out the total parts in one basic mix.
4 (pineapple) + 3 (orange) = 7 total parts.

Step 2: Set up a Part-to-Whole Proportion

Problem: Set up the proportion to find total pineapple juice needed.

Ratio of pineapple to total is 4 to 7. We need to find pineapple (p) when the total is 35.
$\frac{4}{7} = \frac{p}{35}$

Cross multiply: $7p = 140$. Divide by 7: $p = 20$ cups of pineapple juice.

Error to Avoid: Using the Part-to-Part Ratio!

A common mistake is setting up $\frac{4}{3} = \frac{p}{35}$. The denominator on the left must be the WHOLE (4+3=7), not the other part, because the 35 on the right represents the WHOLE mixture!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Scale Drawings & Models

Map Scale: 1 inch = 40 miles
Example Problem Statement:

On a map, the scale is 1 inch equals 40 miles. If the distance between two cities on the map is 3.5 inches, what is the actual distance between the two cities in miles?

Step 1: Write the Scale as a Ratio

Problem: Determine actual distance from map distance.

The scale is a ratio comparing map distance to actual distance. We write this as $\frac{1 \text{ inch}}{40 \text{ miles}}$.

Step 2: Set up Proportion and Solve

• Set your scale ratio equal to the new map measure over the unknown actual distance (d).
• $\frac{1}{40} = \frac{3.5}{d}$
• Cross multiply: $1 \cdot d = 40 \cdot 3.5$
• Solve: $d = 140$
The actual distance is 140 miles.

Error to Avoid: Mixing Up Map and Actual Locations!

Always make sure your units align across the equals sign. If map inches are on top in the first fraction, map inches MUST be on top in the second fraction!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. The Whole

When solving part-to-whole proportions, always add the parts of your ratio together to find the "whole" or total parts.

2. The Setup

Make sure your first ratio compares the PART to the WHOLE, and not one part to another part.

3. Map Scale

A map scale translates distance on the page to real life. Write it as a ratio, like map / actual.

4. Cross Multiply

Once you have your proportion perfectly set up with matching units on the top and bottom, cross multiply to solve!
