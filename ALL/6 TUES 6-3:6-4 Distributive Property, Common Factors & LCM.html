Key Vocabulary Terms

Distributive Property: Multiplying a sum by a number gives the same result as multiplying each addend separately: a(b + c) = a x b + a x c.
greatest common factor (GCF): The largest whole number that divides evenly into two or more numbers.
relatively prime: Numbers that share no common factor other than 1 (for example, 9 and 4).
least common multiple (LCM): The smallest non-zero whole number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.
multiple: The product of a whole number and any non-zero whole number.
prime factorization: Expressing a number as a product of prime numbers (e.g., 12 = 2 x 2 x 3).

Objective 1: Distributive Property & Common Factors (Lesson 6-3)

Example: 36 + 16 = 4(9 + 4)
Example 1: Room Area Equivalence

• Julia's Way: (10 x 8) + (10 x 7) = 80 + 70 = 150 sq ft
• Risa's Way: 10(8 + 7) = 10 x 15 = 150 sq ft
Both ways give 150 because the Distributive Property states that multiplying a sum by a number is the same as multiplying each addend by that number.

Step 1: Find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

List factors for addends 36 and 16:
• Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
• Factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16
The greatest common factor (GCF) is 4.

Step 2: Write Addends as Multiples of the GCF

Express each addend using the GCF as a factor:
• 36 = 4 x 9
• 16 = 4 x 4

Step 3: Apply Distributive Property & Verify Relative Primes

Rewrite the sum: 36 + 16 = (4 x 9) + (4 x 4) = 4(9 + 4).
Notice that 9 and 4 share no factors other than 1, so they are relatively prime.

Visual Equation Breakdown
36 + 16
Rewrite each addend using GCF = 4
(4 x 9) + (4 x 4)
Factor out the GCF using Distributive Property
4(9 + 4)
9 and 4 have no common factor other than 1 (Relatively Prime!)

Error to Avoid: Factoring Out a Number That is NOT the GCF!

If you rewrite 60 + 90 as 3(20 + 30), 3 is a common factor, but it is NOT the GCF! 20 and 30 still share a common factor of 10. Always factor out the GCF (30) so the remaining numbers in parentheses, 30(2 + 3), are relatively prime!

Objective 2: Least Common Multiple (LCM) (Lesson 6-4)

Example: LCM(4, 10) = 20
Method 1: Writing a List of Multiples

To find the LCM of 2 and 12 (Mars & Jupiter orbits):
• Multiples of 2: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14...
• Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36...
The smallest shared non-zero multiple is 12. Thus, LCM(2, 12) = 12.

Method 2: Using Prime Factorization

Find the LCM of 4 and 10:
1. Write prime factorizations:
   4 = 2 x 2
   10 = 2 x 5
2. Take each prime factor the greatest number of times it appears in any number:
   • Factor 2 appears twice (in 4). Write 2 x 2.
   • Factor 5 appears once (in 10). Write 5.
3. Multiply: 2 x 2 x 5 = 20. Thus, LCM(4, 10) = 20.

LCM Method Comparison Matrix
Multiples List Strategy:

Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24...

Multiples of 10: 10, 20, 30, 40...

Prime Factorization Strategy:

4 = 2 x 2

10 = 2 x 5

LCM = 2 x 2 x 5 = 20

Critical Distinction: Don't Confuse GCF and LCM!

• GCF is the greatest factor (smaller than or equal to the original numbers).
• LCM is the least multiple (larger than or equal to the original numbers).

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Turn In

1. Distributive Rule

Multiplying a sum by a number is equivalent to multiplying each addend separately: a(b + c) = ab + ac.

2. GCF Factoring

Always factor out the Greatest Common Factor so the remaining addends are relatively prime.

3. Finding LCM

Use a multiples list or prime factorization to find the smallest shared non-zero multiple.

4. Verify Answers

Double check that your factors divide evenly and your multiples align correctly!
