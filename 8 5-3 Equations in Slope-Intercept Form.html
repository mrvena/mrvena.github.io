Today's Learning Objective

I can identify the slope and y-intercept of a line, use them to graph the line, and write linear equations in slope-intercept form.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Car Rentals

Example Problem: A monthly fee of 20 dollars plus 15 dollars per hour. The y-intercept is the starting fee (20), and the slope is the hourly rate (15).

Saving Money

Example Problem: Starting with 50 dollars in the bank and depositing 10 dollars every week creates a perfect linear equation on a graph.

Temperature Change

Example Problem: A liquid starts at freezing and heats up 2 degrees every minute. Slope-intercept form helps predict exactly when it boils.

Distance Travel

Example Problem: A train is already 100 miles down the track and travels at 60 mph. Slope-intercept equations quickly map out the total trip.

Key Vocabulary Terms

slope-intercept form: A linear equation written in the form y = mx + b.
slope (m): The constant rate of change; the coefficient of x in the equation. Think of it as "rise over run".
y-intercept (b): The constant in the equation; the y-coordinate of the point where the graph crosses the y-axis (0, b).
constant rate: A rate of change that stays exactly the same, creating a straight line on a graph.
coefficient: The number multiplying the variable (like the m next to the x).
initial value: Another way to think of the y-intercept, representing a starting point before any change happens.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Identify Slope and y-intercept

Form: y = mx + b
Example Problem Statement:

Given the linear equation y = 4x - 2, identify the slope and the y-intercept.

Step 1: Identify the Slope (m)

Problem: Identify the slope of y = 4x - 2.

In slope-intercept form (y = mx + b), the slope (m) is the coefficient directly in front of the x.
Looking at the equation, the number attached to x is 4. Therefore, the slope is 4.

Step 2: Identify the y-intercept (b)

Problem: Identify the y-intercept of y = 4x - 2.

The y-intercept (b) is the constant term added or subtracted at the end.
Looking at the equation, the constant is -2. Therefore, the y-intercept is -2. The graph crosses the y-axis at (0, -2).

Breaking Down the Equation
y = 4x - 2
Slope (m) = 4
y-intercept (b) = -2

Error to Avoid: Mixing Up m and b!

Never assume the first number you see is the slope! The slope is always the number multiplying the variable x, regardless of the order they are written in. For example, in y = 7 - 3x, the slope is -3, not 7!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Writing Linear Equations

Write an equation given slope and a point!
Example Problem Statement:

Write an equation of the line in slope-intercept form with a slope of -3 that contains the point (2, 4).

Step 1: Set Up and Substitute

Problem: Equation for line with slope -3 passing through (2, 4).

Start with the slope-intercept form: y = mx + b.
We know the slope (m) is -3. We also have an (x, y) point, which is (2, 4).
Substitute these into the equation: 4 = -3(2) + b

Step 2: Solve for b and Write Equation

• Multiply: 4 = -6 + b
• Add 6 to both sides: 10 = b
Now that you know b is 10, write the final equation using m and b.
The final equation is y = -3x + 10.

Substitution Map
y = mx + b

4 = -3(2) + b

4 = -6 + b
10 = b
Final Equation: y = -3x + 10

Error to Avoid: Swapping x and y!

When plugging an ordered pair (x, y) into the equation y = mx + b, make absolutely sure you put the first number in for x and the second number in for y. It is easy to accidentally plug them in backward!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. The Form

Slope-intercept form is y = mx + b. This is the most common way to write linear equations!

2. The Slope

The letter 'm' represents the slope, which is the constant rate of change multiplying the x variable.

3. The y-intercept

The letter 'b' represents the y-intercept, the starting point where the graph crosses the y-axis at (0, b).

4. Substituting

If you have the slope and an (x, y) point, you can plug them into the equation to solve for the missing b!
