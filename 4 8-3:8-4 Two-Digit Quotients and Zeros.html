Today's Learning Objective

I can divide to find two-digit quotients, and I can correctly place zeros in my quotients.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Sharing Equally

Example: You want to split 96 marbles equally among 4 friends. Division helps you find the exact equal amount for each person.

Planting & Rows

Example: A farmer plants 405 seeds in 4 rows. Division tells you how many go in each row and what is left over as a remainder.

Saving Money

Example: You saved 84 dollars over 3 weeks. Finding the two-digit quotient shows exactly what your weekly savings are.

Grouping Sets

Example: A baker makes 630 cookies to put into 6 boxes. Finding zeros in the quotient keeps all the place values correct!

Key Vocabulary Terms

divide: To separate into equal groups or sets.
quotient: The final answer to a division problem.
estimate: To find a number close to the exact amount to help check your work.
remainder: The amount left over when a number cannot be divided equally.
set: A specific group of items.
regroup: To rearrange amounts into different place values, like changing 1 hundred into 10 tens.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Two-Digit Quotients

Division Expression: 87 ÷ 3
Example Problem Statement: Find 87 ÷ 3.

We will use the standard division steps to find the two-digit quotient.

Step 1: Divide the Tens

Look at the tens place (8). How many times does 3 go into 8 without going over? It goes in 2 times. (2 × 3 = 6).

Step 2: Multiply, Subtract, Compare, and Bring Down

Multiply 2 × 3 to get 6. Subtract 6 from 8 to get 2 left over. Compare: 2 is less than 3, so we are good. Finally, bring down the 7 ones to make 27 ones.

Step 3: Divide the Ones

Now divide 27 ones by 3. 27 ÷ 3 is 9. Write 9 in the ones place of the quotient. There is no remainder! The quotient is 29.

Division Steps
Divide ➔ Multiply ➔ Subtract ➔ Bring Down
29
-----
3 ) 87

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to Bring Down!

Always make sure you bring down the ones digit before dividing a second time. If you forget to bring it down, your quotient will be incorrect!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Zeros in Quotients

Division Expression: 618 ÷ 6
Example Problem Statement: Find 618 ÷ 6.

Sometimes you will find a zero in your quotient if a set cannot be shared equally.

Step 1: Divide the Hundreds

Start with the hundreds. 6 hundreds divided by 6 is 1.

Step 2: Divide the Tens and Regroup

Bring down the 1 ten. You cannot share 1 ten equally into 6 sets. So, you must write a 0 in the quotient above the tens digit. You will regroup this ten into ones!

Step 3: Divide the Ones

Bring down the 8 ones to join the 1 ten, making 18 ones in total. 18 divided by 6 is 3. The final quotient is 103.

Zero in the Quotient
618 ÷ 6

6 ÷ 6 = 1 (Hundreds)
1 ÷ 6 = 0 (Tens)
18 ÷ 6 = 3 (Ones)
Final Quotient: 103

Error to Avoid: Missing Zeros!

Always make sure you write a zero in the quotient when there are not enough tens to divide! If you forget it, your place values will all be wrong!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Divide Equaly

Division is all about sharing numbers into equal-sized sets or groups.

2. Bring Down

Always make sure you bring down the next digit from your original number so you can divide it correctly.

3. Zeros Are Heroes

If a place value cannot be split into your groups, you must write a zero in that place value of your quotient!

4. Check Your Work

You can always check a division problem by multiplying your quotient by the divisor!
