Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Racing & Time

A second-place car reached the finish line 1/2 second behind the winner. A third car was 5/8 second behind the second. Adding these fractions gives the total time gap!

Business Profits

A company has a profit of $4 1/4 million one year and a loss of -$3 1/8 million the next. Adding positive and negative mixed numbers finds the net gain!

Construction

When cutting a 10-foot board to fit a 7 3/8 foot space, a carpenter regroups integers into mixed numbers to subtract accurately.

Baking Adjustments

If a recipe calls for 2 1/3 cups of flour and you only have 1 3/4 cups, subtracting with unlike denominators tells you exactly how much you need to borrow!

Key Vocabulary Terms

simplest form: A fraction is in lowest terms when 1 is the only common factor of the numerator and denominator.
common denominator: A common multiple (like the LCD) used to rename fractions so they can be added or subtracted.
subtraction principle: To subtract a number, you add its opposite. Example: a - b = a + (-b).
regroup: Renaming an integer or mixed number (e.g., changing 6 to 5 7/7) to allow for fraction subtraction.
absolute value: A number's distance from zero. When adding unlike signs, the addend with the greater absolute value determines the sum's sign.
estimate: Rounding mixed numbers to the nearest integer before solving to check if your final answer is reasonable.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Add & Subtract Positive and Negative Fractions (Lesson 5-6)

Example Problem: 3/4 + (-1/6)
Example Problem Statement:

Add the fractions with unlike denominators and unlike signs: 3/4 + (-1/6). Express the sum in lowest terms.

Step 1: Find the LCD

The denominators are 4 and 6.
• Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16...
• Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18...
The Least Common Denominator (LCD) is 12.

Step 2: Rename Fractions & Add

Use the LCD to rename both fractions:
• 3/4 = (3 × 3) / (4 × 3) = 9/12
• -1/6 = (-1 × 2) / (6 × 2) = -2/12
Add the numerators: 9 + (-2) = 7.
The positive addend has the greater absolute value, so the sum is positive 7/12.

Unlike Denominators, Unlike Signs
3/4 9/12
+
-1/6 -2/12
Sum: 7/12

Error to Avoid: Subtracting Wrong with Negatives!

When applying the Subtraction Principle: a - b = a + (-b). If you subtract a negative, it becomes adding a positive! Example: 1/5 - (-2/5) becomes 1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5.

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Add and Subtract Mixed Numbers (Lesson 5-7)

Example Problem: 7 1/3 - 4 5/6
Example Problem Statement:

Subtract the mixed numbers and regroup to solve: 7 1/3 - 4 5/6. Express in simplest form.

Step 1: Estimate & Rename Fractions

Estimate: 7 - 5 = 2.
Find LCD: The LCD of 3 and 6 is 6.
Rename: 7 1/3 = 7 2/6.
Now set up subtraction: 7 2/6 - 4 5/6. Since 2/6 < 5/6, we must regroup!

Step 2: Regroup and Subtract

Regroup 7 2/6:
7 2/6 = 6 + 1 + 2/6 = 6 + 6/6 + 2/6 = 6 8/6.
Now subtract: 6 8/6 - 4 5/6 = 2 3/6.
Simplify the fraction part: 2 3/6 = 2 1/2. (Close to estimate of 2!)

Regrouping Visual Summary
7 2/6 → 6 8/6
Original setup: 7 2/6 - 4 5/6
Regrouped: 6 8/6 - 4 5/6
Final Difference: 2 3/6 = 2 1/2

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to Regroup the Integer!

When you borrow 1 whole to add to your fraction (like 6/6), remember to subtract 1 from your integer part. Don't leave the 7 as a 7!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. LCD First

Always find the Least Common Denominator (LCD) to rename fractions before trying to add or subtract.

2. Subtraction Rule

Apply the Subtraction Principle! Remember that a - b is exactly the same as adding the opposite: a + (-b).

3. Regrouping

If the fraction you are subtracting is larger than your starting fraction, borrow 1 whole from the integer!

4. Simplest Form

Always express your final fraction in simplest form or lowest terms. Double check for common factors!
