Today's Learning Objective

I can multiply decimal numbers together and write placeholder zeros in the product when I need them to place the decimal point correctly.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Buying Snacks

Example: Finding the total cost of buying 0.5 pounds of trail mix when it costs 1.20 dollars per pound.

Building Models

Example: Cutting exactly 0.75 of a block of wood that measures 1.2 centimeters long.

Walking Trails

Example: Figuring out how far you walked if you complete 0.25 of a 3.5 mile nature path.

Electricity

Example: Calculating total energy if a tiny light bulb uses 0.05 kilowatts each hour it stays on.

Key Vocabulary Terms

decimal: A number with one or more digits to the right of the decimal point.
factor: A number that is multiplied by another number.
product: The final result or answer when you multiply two or more numbers.
decimal place: The position of a digit to the right of a decimal point.
placeholder zero: A zero used to hold a place value when there are not enough digits.
hundredths: Two decimal places to the right of the decimal point (e.g., 0.01).
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Multiply Decimals by Decimals

Example Problem: 0.6 x 0.7
Example Problem Statement:

Find the product of 0.6 and 0.7.

Step 1: Multiply Like Whole Numbers

Problem: Find the product of 0.6 and 0.7.

First, ignore the decimal points and multiply the numbers like normal.
Multiply 6 x 7 = 42.

Step 2: Count the Decimal Places

Problem: Find the product of 0.6 and 0.7.

Count the total number of digits to the right of the decimal point in BOTH factors.
0.6 has 1 decimal place. 0.7 has 1 decimal place.
Total decimal places = 2.
Mark off 2 decimal places in your product: 0.42.

Decimal Place Mapping
0.6 (1 place)
x 0.7 (1 place)
0.42 (2 total places)

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to Count Both Factors!

A common mistake is to only look at one factor's decimal places. Always count the decimal places in BOTH the top and bottom factors and add them together to place your final decimal point!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Zeros in the Product

Example Problem: 0.04 x 0.2
Example Problem Statement:

Find the product of 0.04 and 0.2.

Step 1: Multiply Like Whole Numbers

Problem: Find the product of 0.04 and 0.2.

Multiply 4 x 2 = 8.

Step 2: Add Zeros as Placeholders

Problem: Find the product of 0.04 and 0.2.

• Count the decimal places: 0.04 has 2 places. 0.2 has 1 place. That's 3 total places.
• We only have the number 8, which is just 1 digit. We need 3 digits!
• Write two zeros to the left of the 8 to make enough places: 0.008.
The final product is 0.008.

Placeholder Zero Machine
4 x 2 = 8

We need 3 places, but 8 is only 1 place.

Write zeros to the LEFT: 0.008

Error to Avoid: Putting Zeros on the Right!

Always add your placeholder zeros to the left of your multiplied numbers. If you added them to the right, 0.008 would become 0.800, which is incorrect!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Multiply Normal

Start by multiplying your factors as if they were just regular whole numbers.

2. Count Both

Count the decimal places in the first factor and the second factor. Don't forget one!

3. Add Places

Add the number of decimal places together. This is the total number of places your product needs.

4. Placeholder Zeros

If your product doesn't have enough digits, write zeros to the left to place the decimal point correctly.
