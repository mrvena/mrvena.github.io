Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Viral Trends

Example: A video is shared by 5 people, then each of them shares it with 5 more people. The views multiply exponentially forming a geometric sequence.

Bouncing Balls

Example: When dropped, a ball loses a fraction of its height on each bounce. We use geometric sequences to calculate exact rebound heights over time.

Tournaments

Example: In a bracket tournament, the number of teams gets cut in half every round (64, 32, 16). This creates a shrinking geometric sequence.

Fractals in Design

Example: Creating complex geometric art, like Sierpinski's triangle, involves repeating shapes at a constant ratio to form endless patterns.

Key Vocabulary Terms

sequence: An ordered list of numbers that often follows a specific pattern or rule.
geometric sequence: A sequence where each term is found by multiplying the previous term by a constant number.
common ratio (r): The constant multiplier in a geometric sequence, found by dividing a term by the preceding term.
term (a_n): A single number or value within the sequence. The first term is written as a_1.
nth term formula: A formula that helps you jump to any specific position (n) in the sequence directly: a_n = a_1(r)^(n-1)
recursive formula: A rule for finding a new term solely based on the term immediately before it: a_n = r * a_(n-1)
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Finding Ratios and the Next Term

Example Sequence: 3, 12, 48, 192, ...
Example Problem Statement:

Determine the common ratio of the sequence 3, 12, 48, 192. Then, find the value of the 5th term in the sequence.

Step 1: Find the Common Ratio (r)

Problem: Find the common ratio of 3, 12, 48, 192.

The common ratio is found by dividing any term by the term immediately before it.
Divide the second term (12) by the first term (3): 12 / 3 = 4.
You can check this with the next terms: 48 / 12 = 4. So, r = 4.

Step 2: Find the Next Term

Problem: Find the 5th term in the sequence.

Multiply the last known term (the 4th term, 192) by our common ratio (4).
192 * 4 = 768. The 5th term is 768.

Error to Avoid: Subtracting instead of Dividing!

Geometric sequences rely entirely on multiplication and division. To find the pattern, make sure you divide the second term by the first term, do not subtract them!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Evaluating the Nth Term Formula

Example Formula: a_n = 4(3)^(n-1)
Example Problem Statement:

Evaluate the geometric sequence formula a_n = 4(3)^(n-1) to find the 4th term in the sequence (a_4).

Step 1: Understand the Formula

Problem: Find a_4 when a_n = 4(3)^(n-1).

In this formula, 4 is the first term (a_1), 3 is the common ratio (r), and n is the position you want to find. Since we want the 4th term, n = 4.

Step 2: Substitute and Solve

• Substitute 4 for n in the exponent: a_4 = 4 * (3)^(4-1)
• Subtract in the exponent first: a_4 = 4 * (3)^3
• Solve the exponent: (3)^3 = 3 * 3 * 3 = 27
• Multiply: 4 * 27 = 108
The final output for the 4th term is 108.

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Sequences

A geometric sequence is a list of numbers where you always multiply to get from one term to the very next term.

2. Common Ratio

Find the common ratio (r) by dividing any term by the term that came right before it in the list.

3. Terms (a_n)

The letter 'a' stands for term, and the small number 'n' tells you exactly which position in the list you are looking at.

4. Exponents First

When using the nth term formula, always subtract the exponent (n-1) and solve the power before you multiply by the first term!
