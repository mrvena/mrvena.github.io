Today's Learning Objective

I can divide four-digit numbers to find the quotient and remainder, and I can use the order of operations to solve math problems with more than one step!

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Packing Supplies

Problem: A business has 2154 pens to pack equally into boxes of 8.

Solution: We divide 2154 by 8 to find they need 269 full boxes with 2 pens left over as a remainder.

Buying Tickets

Problem: You buy 4 tickets for $15 each, plus a $5 fee.

Solution: The order of operations tells us to multiply 4 by 15 first ($60), then add the $5 fee to get $65 total!

Event Seating

Problem: 1045 people are attending a show with 5 equal sections.

Solution: We divide the total number of people by 5 to find there are exactly 209 people per section with no remainder.

Computer Coding

Problem: A computer needs to calculate 10 + 20 / 2.

Solution: Computers follow the order of operations! They divide 20 by 2 first (10), then add 10 to get 20.

Key Vocabulary Terms

dividend: The number that is being divided into parts.
quotient: The answer to a division problem.
remainder: The amount left over that cannot be divided evenly.
order of operations: The mathematical rules for what steps to do first.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Dividing Greater Dividends

Example Rule: 4527 ÷ 5
Example Problem Statement:

Find the quotient and remainder when you divide 4527 by 5.

Step 1: Divide the Hundreds and Tens

Problem: Start dividing 4527 by 5 from left to right.

5 cannot go into 4, so look at 45. 45 ÷ 5 = 9. So we place a 9 in the hundreds place.
Next, drop the 2. 5 cannot go into 2, so we place a 0 in the tens place.

Step 2: Divide the Ones and Find Remainder

Problem: Drop the 7 to make 27.

27 ÷ 5 = 5 with some left over. We place a 5 in the ones place.
5 × 5 = 25. Subtracting 25 from 27 leaves a remainder of 2. The final answer is 905 R 2.

Long Division Layout
905 R 2
5 ) 4527

Error to Avoid: Forgetting the Zero!

If your divisor cannot go into the next digit (like 5 trying to go into 2), you MUST write a zero in the quotient before bringing down the next digit!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Order of Operations

Example Problem: 14 + 36 ÷ 4 - 2 × 3
Example Problem Statement:

Find the value of the expression using the order of operations: 14 + 36 ÷ 4 - 2 × 3

Step 1: Multiply or Divide (Left to Right)

Problem: Look for any multiplication or division first.

We see 36 ÷ 4 and 2 × 3.
36 ÷ 4 = 9.
2 × 3 = 6.
Now rewrite the problem: 14 + 9 - 6.

Step 2: Add or Subtract (Left to Right)

Problem: With just addition and subtraction left, work left to right.

First add 14 + 9 = 23.
Then subtract: 23 - 6 = 17.
The final value is 17.

Order of Operations Steps
14 + 36 ÷ 4 - 2 × 3

14 + 9 - 6

23 - 6
Final Value: 17

Error to Avoid: Just Reading Left to Right!

Never just read a math expression like a book. You MUST check for multiplying or dividing anywhere in the problem before you add or subtract.

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Divide Place by Place

Dividing a 4-digit number is the same as a 3-digit number. Start from the left (thousands or hundreds) and divide each place value!

2. Don't Forget Zeros

If your divisor cannot go into a digit you brought down, place a zero in the quotient for that place value.

3. Multiply/Divide First

In any math expression, you MUST multiply and divide before you add and subtract. Go left to right!

4. Add/Subtract Last

Once only addition and subtraction are left, just solve the problem reading left to right.
