Key Vocabulary Terms

nonzero place: The leftmost place value in a number that contains a digit other than 0.
front-end estimation: Estimating by adding the values of the front digits in the greatest nonzero place.
rounding: Adjusting numbers to nearby place values (like nearest tenth or whole) for quick estimation.
hundredth: One part out of 100 equal parts (0.01), two places to the right of the decimal point.
sum: The total result obtained from adding numbers together.
regroup: Exchanging 10 hundredths as 1 tenth or 10 tenths as 1 one when adding place-value columns.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Instruction

Objective 1: Estimate Decimal Sums (Lesson 10-3)

Example: 1.35 + 3.1 + 0.75 ≈ 5
Method 1: Front-End Estimation

Identify the front digits in the greatest nonzero place of each number, then add:
1.00 + 3.0 + 0.70 = 4.7 (about 4.7 or 5).

Method 2: Rounding Method

Round each decimal to the nearest tenth (the greatest nonzero place of the least number):
1.35 → 1.4, 3.1 → 3.1, 0.75 → 0.8.

Step 3: Add Rounded Numbers

1.4 + 3.1 + 0.8 = 5.3 (The duathlon is approximately 5 miles long).

Step 4: Compare Estimation Techniques

rounding often gives a closer estimate than front-end estimation because it considers the next place value.

Error to Avoid: Mixing Up Front-End and Rounding!

When using front-end estimation, look only at the greatest nonzero front digits. When using rounding, check the digit to the right to decide whether to round up or keep the digit!

Objective 2: Add Decimals to Hundredths (Lesson 10-5)

Example: 0.37 + 1.26 = 1.63
Step 1: Line Up Decimal Points

Line up the decimal points and align place-value columns (ones, tenths, hundredths).

1
Step 2: Add Hundredths & Regroup

• 7 hundredths + 6 hundredths = 13 hundredths.
• Write 3 in hundredths place and regroup 1 tenth above the tenths column.

2
Step 3: Add Tenths & Ones

• 1 (carried) + 3 + 2 = 6 tenths.
• 0 + 1 = 1 one.
• Bring down the decimal point directly aligned.

Step 4: Final Sum

0.37 + 1.26 = 1.63 (Brian bought 1.63 pounds of onions).

Critical Error: Always Align Decimal Points!

Never align numbers by their rightmost digit if they have different decimal places! Add placeholder zeros (like 3.6 → 3.60) to keep columns properly aligned before adding.

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Turn In

1. Front-End Estimation

Add the values of the front digits in the greatest nonzero place for a quick estimate.

2. Rounding Sums

Round decimals to the nearest tenth or whole number for a closer estimate.

3. Align Decimals

Always line up decimal points and place-value columns before adding!

4. Regroup Correctly

Regroup 10 hundredths as 1 tenth or 10 tenths as 1 one as you add columns right-to-left.
