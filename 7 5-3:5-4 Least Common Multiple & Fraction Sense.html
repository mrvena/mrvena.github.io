Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Sports Practice

Carlos has soccer practice every 5 days and swim practice every 8 days. Finding the LCM of 5 and 8 shows both practices overlap every 40 days!

Baking & Recipes

A baker compares 3/10 cup sugar and 4/15 cup flour. Finding the Least Common Denominator (LCD = 30) allows easy comparison of ingredients!

Carpentry & Design

A carpenter measures a gap of 2/25 of an inch. Using fraction sense, he knows 2/25 is close to 0 inches, so no spacer is required!

Weather & Elevation

Overnight temperature dropped -15/16 of a degree. Recognizing -15/16 is close to -1 whole degree helps meteorologists quickly estimate climate shifts!

Key Vocabulary Terms

multiple: The product of a number and any non-zero whole number (e.g., multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12...).
common multiple: A multiple that is shared by two or more whole numbers.
least common multiple (LCM): The smallest non-zero common multiple of two or more numbers.
least common denominator (LCD): The least common multiple (LCM) of the denominators of two or more fractions.
benchmark: Standard reference numbers (like -1, -1/2, 0, 1/2, 1) used to estimate fraction values.
compatible numbers: Numbers that are easy to divide or compare mentally when estimating fraction benchmarks.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) & LCD (Lesson 5-3)

Example Problem: LCM of 4, 6, and 10
Example Problem Statement:

Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of 4, 6, and 10 to determine when three recurring events will occur on the exact same day.

Method 1: List the Multiples

List multiples for each number until you find the first common value:
• Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, 52, 56, 60...
• Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60...
• Multiples of 10: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60...

Method 2: Prime Factorization

Write prime factors using maximum powers:
• 4 = 2 × 2 = 22
• 6 = 2 × 3
• 10 = 2 × 5
LCM = 22 × 3 × 5 = 4 × 3 × 5 = 60.

Finding Least Common Multiple / Denominator
4 2 × 2
6 2 × 3
10 2 × 5
Calculation: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 60

Error to Avoid: Confusing Factors with Multiples!

Factors are numbers you multiply together to get a product (smaller or equal). Multiples are products of a number and whole numbers (equal or larger)!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Fraction Sense - Benchmarks -1, -1/2, 0, 1/2, 1 (Lesson 5-4)

Example Problem: 11/24 & -13/14
Example Problem Statement:

Determine whether 11/24 is closest to 0, 1/2, or 1, and whether -13/14 is closest to -1, -1/2, or 0 using benchmark rules.

Rule 1: Close to 1/2 or -1/2

Double the absolute value of the numerator. If it is about equal to the denominator, the fraction is close to 1/2 (or -1/2).
Example: For 11/24, 2 × 11 = 22, which is very close to 24. So 11/24 is close to 1/2.

Rule 2: Close to 1 or -1

If the absolute value of the numerator is almost equal to the denominator, the fraction is close to 1 (or -1).
Example: For -13/14, |-13| = 13, which is almost equal to 14. So -13/14 is close to -1.

Benchmark Reference Summary
Benchmark Fractions
Close to 0: Numerator much smaller than Denominator
Close to 1/2 or -1/2: Numerator is about half of Denominator
Close to 1 or -1: Numerator is almost equal to Denominator
Examples: 11/24 → 1/2 | -13/14 → -1

Error to Avoid: Ignoring Negative Signs!

Always check if the fraction is negative! For example, -11/12 is close to -1, NOT positive 1!

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. LCM Method

List multiples or use prime factorization with highest exponents to find the LCM!

2. LCD Concept

The LCD is simply the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of fraction denominators.

3. Benchmark -1/2 & 1/2

If double the numerator is about equal to the denominator, the fraction is close to 1/2 or -1/2.

4. Benchmark -1 & 1

If the numerator is about equal to the denominator, the fraction is close to 1 or -1.
