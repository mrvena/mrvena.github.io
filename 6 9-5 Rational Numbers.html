Today's Learning Objective

I can represent positive and negative rational numbers on a number line, convert between rational fractions and decimals, and identify the opposite of any rational number.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Baseball Analytics (WAR)

Example: Baseball teams use Wins Above Replacement (WAR) to measure player value. A WAR of 0 is average, +3.5 shows positive wins added, and -1.5 shows losses compared to an average player.

Ocean Depth & Elevation

Example: Marine scientists track whales diving 302.25 ft or 425.5 ft below sea level using negative rational numbers relative to 0 at the ocean surface.

Finance & Stock Prices

Example: Financial markets track daily changes in share prices using decimals and fractions, such as a price drop of -3.5 points or -3 1/2 points.

Weather & Temperature

Example: Meteorologists record winter temperatures between whole degrees on a thermometer number line, like -2.5 degrees Celsius below freezing.

Key Vocabulary & Number Sets

rational number: Any number you can write as a fraction a/b or -a/b where a and b are whole numbers and b is not 0.
whole numbers: Counting numbers starting from 0 (such as 0, 4, 9, 17, 100). All whole numbers are also integers and rational numbers.
integers: Whole numbers and their opposites (such as -22, -5, -3, -1, 0, 4). All integers are rational numbers.
opposite: Two numbers that are the exact same distance from 0 on a number line, but on opposite sides (for example, 1/2 and -1/2).
equivalent forms: Every rational number can be written both as a fraction and as a decimal (for example, 1/2 = 0.5 and 0.25 = 1/4).
number line: A visual line showing 0 at the center, positive rational numbers to the right of 0, and negative rational numbers to the left of 0.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Rational Numbers on a Number Line

Key Form: a/b or -a/b (b ≠ 0)
Example Problem Statement:

A marine biologist tracks two sea turtles relative to the ocean surface (0 meters). Turtle A is swimming at -2.5 meters, and Turtle B is resting on a rock at 1.5 meters. Identify the location of both rational numbers on a number line.

Step 1: Locate Negative Rational Numbers (Left of 0)

Problem: Locate -2.5 on a number line.

Start at 0 and move to the left because the number is negative. Pass -1 and -2, then move halfway between -2 and -3. That point is -2.5 (or -2 1/2).

Step 2: Locate Positive Rational Numbers (Right of 0)

Problem: Locate 1.5 on a number line.

Start at 0 and move to the right because the number is positive. Pass 1, then move halfway between 1 and 2. That point is 1.5 (or 1 1/2).

Rational Number Line Visual
Notice that -2.5 is halfway between -2 and -3 (NOT between -1 and -2!).

Error to Avoid: Counting the Wrong Direction on the Negative Side!

To the left of 0, numbers move further negative as you go left (-1, then -2, then -3). A number like -2.5 is between -2 and -3, NOT between -1 and -2!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Decimals, Fractions, and Opposites

Example Number: -3 1/4
Example Problem Statement:

Write the rational number -3 1/4 as a decimal, and then find the opposite of -3 1/4.

Step 1: Convert the Rational Number to a Decimal

Problem: Write -3 1/4 as a decimal.

Keep the whole number part (-3). Convert the fraction 1/4 to a decimal by dividing 1 by 4 (1 ÷ 4 = 0.25). Combine them to get -3.25.

Step 2: Find the Opposite of the Rational Number

Problem: Find the opposite of -3 1/4.

• The opposite of a number is the same distance from 0 on the opposite side.
• Since -3 1/4 is negative, its opposite is positive: 3 1/4 (or 3.25).

Fraction-Decimal & Opposite Converter
Original: -3 1/4

Decimal Form: -3.25

Opposite Value: 3 1/4 or 3.25

Error to Avoid: Losing the Negative Sign When Converting!

When converting a negative mixed number like -3 1/4 into a decimal, make sure your final decimal still has the negative sign (-3.25)! Only drop the negative sign when asked for the opposite.

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Rational Numbers

Any number that can be written as a fraction a/b or -a/b (with b ≠ 0) is a rational number—including all whole numbers and integers!

2. Number Lines

Positive rational numbers sit to the right of 0, and negative rational numbers sit to the left of 0.

3. Opposites

Opposite rational numbers are the exact same distance from 0 on opposite sides of the number line (like 3/4 and -3/4).

4. Two Forms

Every rational number can be written both as a fraction and as a decimal, such as 1/2 = 0.5 or -3 1/4 = -3.25.
