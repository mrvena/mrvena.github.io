Today's Learning Objective

I can use a number line to compare rational numbers and correctly arrange them in order from least to greatest or greatest to least.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Weather & Temperature

Example: A glaciologist studies temperatures that drop below zero. Comparing -7.5°F and -10.25°F is essential to track climate changes.

Sea Level Elevation

Example: Submarines and scuba divers measure depths below sea level as negative numbers to know who is deeper in the ocean.

Bank Balances

Example: If an account has a negative balance, understanding rational numbers helps a person know exactly how much they owe compared to someone else.

Sports Scoring

Example: In golf, a score of -2 is better than a score of -1. Comparing these negative numbers is how tournament winners are decided.

Key Vocabulary Terms

rational number: Any number that can be written as a fraction, including decimals, integers, and negative numbers.
number line: A straight line with numbers placed at equal intervals, helping us see which numbers are greater or lesser.
least to greatest: Ordering numbers starting from the smallest value and going up to the highest value.
negative number: A number less than zero, plotted to the left of zero on a horizontal number line.
positive number: A number greater than zero, plotted to the right of zero on a horizontal number line.
compare: Determining if one number is greater than (>), less than (<), or equal to (=) another number.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Comparing Rational Numbers

Example Comparison: -4.5 and -2.2
Example Problem Statement:

Compare the rational numbers -4.5 and -2.2. Which of these numbers is greater?

Step 1: Visualize the Number Line

Problem: Which is greater, -4.5 or -2.2?

A number is always greater than a number to its left. We need to plot both -4.5 and -2.2 on a number line to see their locations compared to zero.

Step 2: Compare Their Positions

Problem: Which is greater, -4.5 or -2.2?

-4.5 is further to the left (further away from zero) on the number line than -2.2.
Because -2.2 is to the right of -4.5, -2.2 is the greater number.

-4.5 < -2.2

Error to Avoid: Ignoring the Negative Sign!

It is easy to think -4.5 is greater because the digit 4 is bigger than 2. But with negative numbers, the number that looks "bigger" is actually further to the left on the number line, making it lesser!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Ordering Rational Numbers

Example Problem: 0, -1.5, 2.75
Example Problem Statement:

Order the rational numbers 0, -1.5, and 2.75 from least to greatest.

Step 1: Check Number Types

Problem: Order 0, -1.5, and 2.75.

A negative number is always less than zero and less than a positive number.

Step 2: Place in Order

• The only negative number is -1.5, so it goes furthest to the left (it is the least).
• The number 0 goes in the middle.
• The positive number, 2.75, goes furthest to the right (it is the greatest).
The order from least to greatest is -1.5, 0, 2.75.

Ordering Sequence
-1.5 0 2.75
Least Middle Greatest

Error to Avoid: Reading Directions Too Fast!

Always double check if the directions say "least to greatest" or "greatest to least." Entering your answers backward is a very common mistake!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Number Line

A number is always greater than a number to its left on a horizontal number line.

2. Positive vs Negative

A positive number is always greater than a negative number, no matter the digits.

3. Least to Greatest

When ordering least to greatest, place the number furthest left on the number line first.

4. Where is Zero?

Zero is less than all positive numbers, but greater than all negative numbers.
