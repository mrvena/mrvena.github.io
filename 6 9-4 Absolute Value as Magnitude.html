Today's Learning Objective

I can compare and order positive and negative integers, and use absolute value to understand size, distance, and magnitude in real-world situations.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Sea Level Elevations

Example: A diver at -30 feet and a bird at 20 feet. Absolute value helps us understand who is a greater distance away from sea level (zero).

Bank Debts & Money

Example: Owing 50 dollars (-50) is a larger debt than owing 20 dollars (-20), even though -50 is a smaller integer! Magnitude tells us the size of the debt.

Extreme Temperatures

Example: A temperature of -15 degrees is colder than -2 degrees. Comparing absolute values tells us how far the temperature has dropped from zero.

Sports & Game Scores

Example: A football team might lose 8 yards on a play. The magnitude of that change (8 yards) represents the size of the loss!

Key Vocabulary Terms

absolute value: The distance of a number from zero on a number line, always written as a positive number or zero.
magnitude: The size or amount of a number, regardless of its sign. Represented by its absolute value.
integer: A whole number that can be positive, negative, or zero.
distance: How far apart two things are; distance is always a positive value, which is why absolute value is used.
inequality: A mathematical sentence showing that two quantities are not equal, using < or >.
ground level / sea level: In real-world word problems, this usually represents the number zero (0) on a vertical number line.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Comparing Integers vs. Magnitudes

Focus: Size vs. Actual Value
Example Problem Statement:

In a new neighborhood, technicians run fiber optic cables below ground and power lines above ground. The fiber optic cables are buried 4 feet deep (-4). The power lines are 15 feet high (15). Which are at a greater height? Which are closer to ground level (0)?

Step 1: Compare the Actual Integers

Question 1: Which is at a greater height?

To find the greater height, compare the actual integers. -4 is less than 15 (-4 < 15).
A height of 15 is greater than a depth of -4. The power lines are at a greater height.

Step 2: Compare the Absolute Values (Magnitudes)

Question 2: Which is closer to ground level?

To find which is closer to zero, we look at the distance, or magnitude. We compare their absolute values: |-4| and |15|.
Since 4 < 15, a magnitude of 4 is smaller. The fiber optic cables are closer to ground level.

Distance from Zero Mapping
Fiber Optic (-4)
|-4| = 4
Distance to 0
Power Lines (15)
|15| = 15
4 is a smaller distance than 15!

Error to Avoid: Confusing the Integer Value with its Magnitude!

Be careful! The integer -50 is smaller than -20, but the magnitude |-50| is GREATER than |-20|. When asked about distance or size, always use absolute value!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Using Inequalities for Magnitude

Focus: |a| < |b|
Example Problem Statement:

Write the correct inequality symbol (<, >, or =) to compare the magnitudes of -18 and 15.

Step 1: Write the Absolute Values

Problem: Compare |-18| and |15|.

First, find the absolute value of each integer by determining its distance from zero.

Step 2: Compare the Positive Magnitudes

• |-18| = 18
• |15| = 15
• Compare the results: 18 is greater than 15, so 18 > 15.
Therefore, the final inequality is |-18| > |15|.

Inequality Comparison Machine

|-18| > |15|

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to Convert to Absolute Value First!

If the problem asks you to compare actual values, -18 < 15 is correct. But if the problem has absolute value bars (or asks to compare magnitude), you MUST compare their positive distances!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Absolute Value

Absolute value is simply the distance a number is from zero. Distance is always a positive number!

2. Magnitude

Magnitude means the "size" of a number. When asked for magnitude, you are finding its absolute value.

3. Inequalities

When comparing magnitudes using < or >, always convert both negative and positive integers to their absolute values first!

4. Word Problems

Words like "debt", "below sea level", and "loss" represent negative numbers, but words like "farther" and "greater size" look at magnitude.
