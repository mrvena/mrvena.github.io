Today's Learning Objective

I can multiply decimals by powers of 10 by moving the decimal point, and I can multiply decimals by whole numbers using repeated addition or the distributive property.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Science & Magnification

Example Problem: A plant cell is 0.025 mm wide. A microscope magnifies it 1000 times. How wide does it appear?

Shopping & Money

Example Problem: You buy 4 packs of trading cards that cost $1.25 each. How much do you spend in total?

Building & Materials

Example Problem: A builder uses 100 pieces of wood that are each 0.45 meters long. What is the total length?

Weight & Packaging

Example Problem: A single metal bolt weighs 0.12 pounds. What is the weight of 10^3 bolts?

Key Vocabulary Terms

power of 10: Numbers like 10, 100, and 1000 that can be formed by multiplying 10 by itself.
exponent: A number that tells how many times the base (like 10) is used as a factor.
repeated addition: Adding the exact same number multiple times, which is the same as multiplying.
Distributive Property: Breaking apart a number to multiply its parts separately, then adding the products together.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Multiply by Powers of 10

Example Problem: 0.814 x 100
Example Problem Statement:

Problem: Multiply 0.814 by 100.

Step 1: Count the Zeros or Look at the Exponent

Problem: Multiply 0.814 by 100.

Look at the power of 10. The number 100 has 2 zeros. If the problem used an exponent like 10^2, the exponent would also be 2.

Step 2: Move the Decimal Point

Problem: Multiply 0.814 by 100.

To multiply by a power of 10, move the decimal point to the right as many places as there are zeros.
Move the decimal in 0.814 to the right 2 places.

Decimal Shift Mapping
0.814 x 100 = ?
0 . 8 1 . 4
81.4

Error to Avoid: Not Adding Zeros!

If there are not enough places in the number when you are moving the decimal to the right, you must write one or more zeros to the right of the last digit. Example: 3.5 x 100 = 350.

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Multiply a Decimal by a Whole Number

Example Problem: 3 x 0.24
Example Problem Statement:

Problem: Find the product of 3 and 0.24 using properties of multiplication.

Step 1: Break apart the decimal

Problem: Find 3 x 0.24.

Break apart 0.24 into tenths and hundredths. That gives us 0.20 + 0.04.

Step 2: Use the Distributive Property

• Multiply the whole number by each part separately.
• Find how many tenths: 3 x 0.20 = 0.60
• Find how many hundredths: 3 x 0.04 = 0.12
• Add the sums: 0.60 + 0.12 = 0.72
The final product is 0.72.

Distributive Property Machine
3 x 0.24

(3 x 0.20) + (3 x 0.04)

0.60 + 0.12
Final Product: 0.72

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Shifting Right

When you multiply by a power of 10, the decimal point always moves to the right. The number gets bigger!

2. Counting Zeros

The exponent or the number of zeros tells you exactly how many places to move the decimal point. Add zeros to the end if you need more spaces!

3. Repeated Addition

Multiplying a decimal by a whole number can be modeled by adding the decimal to itself that many times.

4. Distributive Property

You can break a decimal apart into tenths and hundredths, multiply each part separately, and add them back together.
