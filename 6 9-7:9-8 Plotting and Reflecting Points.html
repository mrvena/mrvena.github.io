Today's Learning Objective

I can use the signs of coordinates to locate and plot points on a coordinate plane, and I can recognize when points are reflections across the x-axis, the y-axis, or both.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Mapping & GPS

Example: GPS systems use coordinates (latitude and longitude) to pinpoint your exact location on the earth.

City Planning

Example: Many cities are built on a grid system where streets and avenues act like an x-axis and y-axis to help you find an address.

Video Game Design

Example: Programmers place characters, items, and walls on the screen by giving the computer specific coordinate points.

Architecture

Example: Blueprints use coordinate planes to map out the exact points where walls and corners should be built.

Key Vocabulary Terms

coordinate plane: A grid made of a horizontal x-axis and a vertical y-axis.
origin: The point (0, 0) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect.
quadrant: One of the four sections the coordinate plane is divided into, numbered counterclockwise.
ordered pair: A pair of numbers (x, y) used to describe a location on the coordinate plane.
x-axis & y-axis: The horizontal line is the x-axis, and the vertical line is the y-axis.
reflection: Flipping a point across a line so that the point and its reflection are the same distance from the line.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Plotting Points and Quadrants

Example Point: (4, -5)
Example Problem Statement:

A point is given as the ordered pair (4, -5). Describe how to plot this point, and identify the quadrant in which this point is located.

Step 1: Move along the x-axis

Problem: Plot (4, -5).

Start at the origin (0, 0). The x-coordinate is 4 (a positive number). Move 4 units to the right along the x-axis.

Step 2: Move along the y-axis and find the Quadrant

Problem: Continue to plot (4, -5) and find the quadrant.

From that spot, the y-coordinate is -5 (a negative number). Move 5 units down. The point is placed in the bottom-right section, which is known as Quadrant IV.

Quadrant Mapping Reference
Quadrant II (-x, y)
Quadrant I (x, y)
Quadrant III (-x, -y)
Quadrant IV (x, -y)
(4, -5) goes here!

Error to Avoid: Moving Up/Down First!

Always remember to crawl before you walk! Start at the origin and move left/right (x-axis) BEFORE you move up/down (y-axis).

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Reflections of Points

Example Reflected Point: (-2, 6)
Example Problem Statement:

Reflect the point (-2, 6) across the x-axis. What are the coordinates of the new reflected point?

Step 1: Identify the Axis of Reflection

Problem: Reflect (-2, 6) across the x-axis.

A reflection flips a point across a line. If we reflect across the x-axis, the x-coordinate stays exactly the same, but the y-coordinate becomes the opposite.

Step 2: Change the Coordinate

• Keep the x-coordinate the same: -2 remains -2.
• Change the y-coordinate to its opposite: 6 becomes -6.
The final reflected point is (-2, -6).

Reflection Rules

Across x-axis: Same x, opposite y.
(x, y) → (x, -y)

Across y-axis: Opposite x, same y.
(x, y) → (-x, y)

Across BOTH axes: Opposite x, opposite y.
(x, y) → (-x, -y)

Error to Avoid: Changing the Wrong Coordinate!

It is easy to get mixed up. If you are reflecting across the x-axis, the x-value stays safe and the y-value flips! If you reflect across the y-axis, the y-value stays safe!

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. X-Axis First

Always plot the x-coordinate first by moving left or right from the origin before moving up or down.

2. Four Quadrants

The quadrants are numbered I, II, III, and IV going counterclockwise starting from the top-right corner.

3. Reflecting

Reflecting across an axis is like looking in a mirror. The point flips to the opposite side of that axis line!

4. Opposites

Reflecting across the x-axis changes the y-coordinate. Reflecting across the y-axis changes the x-coordinate.
