Today's Learning Objective

I can identify a linear function by checking for a constant rate of change, and I can calculate the slope of any line from two points using the rise over run formula.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Mountain Hiking

When climbing a mountain at a steady pace, your elevation gain per hour represents a constant rate of change (a linear function).

Saving Money

If you deposit the exact same amount of money into a savings account every week, the total balance grows along a straight line with a constant slope.

Roofing & Ramps

Builders use slope (rise over run) to calculate the steepness of a house roof or to make sure a wheelchair ramp is safe and accessible.

Downloading Files

If a video game downloads at 50 megabytes per second, the download speed is the constant slope of the data transfer.

Key Vocabulary Terms

linear function: A function whose graph creates a straight, non-vertical line.
rate of change: A ratio comparing how much one quantity changes relative to another.
slope (m): The constant ratio of the vertical change to the horizontal change between any two points on a line.
rise: The vertical change (the change in the y-values) on a graph.
run: The horizontal change (the change in the x-values) on a graph.
undefined slope: A vertical line has an undefined slope because the run (change in x) is zero.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Identifying Linear Functions

Core Concept: Constant Rate of Change
Example Problem Statement:

Does the following table represent a linear function? If so, what is the constant rate of change?
Ordered Pairs (x, y): (0, 10), (3, 25), (6, 40), and (9, 55).

Step 1: Find the change in the y-values (Rise)

Look at how the outputs (y-values) change from one row to the next: 10 to 25, 25 to 40, and 40 to 55.
The change in y is a constant +15.

Step 2: Find the change in the x-values (Run)

Look at how the inputs (x-values) change from one row to the next: 0 to 3, 3 to 6, and 6 to 9.
The change in x is a constant +3.

Step 3: Calculate the Rate of Change

Ratio = (Change in y) / (Change in x).
15 / 3 = 5. Since this ratio is constant everywhere, the relation is a linear function with a slope of 5.

Error to Avoid: Only Checking the First Pair!

Do not just check the rate of change for the very first pair of coordinates. You must check every pair in the table to make sure the rate stays constant the entire way down!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Calculating Slope From Two Points

Slope Formula: m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)
Example Problem Statement:

Find the exact slope of the line that contains the points (2, 6) and (6, 34).

Step 1: Identify your coordinate values

Label your first point as (x₁, y₁) and your second point as (x₂, y₂).

For (2, 6), x₁ = 2 and y₁ = 6.
For (6, 34), x₂ = 6 and y₂ = 34.

Step 2: Substitute into the Slope Formula

• Set up the fraction: (y₂ - y₁) over (x₂ - x₁)
• Substitute the numbers: (34 - 6) over (6 - 2)
• Subtract the numerator: 34 - 6 = 28
• Subtract the denominator: 6 - 2 = 4
• Divide to simplify: 28 / 4 = 7. The slope is 7!

Slope Formula Machine
m = (34 - 6) / (6 - 2)

m = 28 / 4

Final Slope (m): 7

Error to Avoid: Putting the X's on Top!

Always remember that slope is Rise over Run. The vertical change (y-values) must be calculated on the top of the fraction, and the horizontal change (x-values) goes on the bottom.

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. Linear Functions

A function is only linear if it has a constant rate of change between every single point on its line.

2. Rise over Run

Slope is calculated as the vertical change (the Rise or change in y) over the horizontal change (the Run or change in x).

3. The Formula

Always make sure to keep the order of your coordinates consistent when using the formula m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁).

4. Zero vs Undefined

A horizontal line has a slope of 0. A vertical line has an undefined slope because you cannot divide by zero!
