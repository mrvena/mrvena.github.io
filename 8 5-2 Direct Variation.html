Today's Learning Objective

I can identify and interpret direct variations, figure out the constant of variation, and write and solve direct variation equations.

Section 1

Why We Need This Math & Key Vocabulary

Hourly Wages

Example: If you earn $15 per hour at a job, the money you make varies directly with how many hours you work.

Recipe Scaling

Example: A recipe calls for 2 cups of flour for every 1 cup of sugar. As you double the sugar, you must directly double the flour.

Distance & Speed

Example: Driving at a constant 65 mph, the total miles you cover varies directly with the number of hours you've been on the road.

Currency Exchange

Example: When converting dollars to euros at a set exchange rate, the euros you get varies directly with the dollars you trade.

Key Vocabulary Terms

direct variation: A linear function that can be written in the form y = kx.
constant of variation: The nonzero constant 'k' in a direct variation equation. It is found by dividing y by x (k = y/x).
linear function: A mathematical relationship that creates a straight line when graphed.
origin: The point (0,0) on a coordinate plane. Every direct variation graph passes through the origin.
slope: The constant rate of change of a line. In a direct variation, the constant of variation is exactly the slope!
proportional: When two quantities grow or shrink at the exact same constant rate.
Section 2 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 1: Identifying Direct Variation & The Constant

Core Formula: k = y / x
Example Problem Statement:

Tell whether the relation given by the ordered pairs (3, 12), (5, 20), and (7, 28) represents a direct variation. If so, find the constant of variation and write the equation.

Step 1: Check the Ratio of y to x

Problem: Determine if (3, 12), (5, 20), and (7, 28) is a direct variation.

Divide the y-value by the x-value for each ordered pair to see if the ratio (k) is constant.
12 / 3 = 4
20 / 5 = 4
28 / 7 = 4
Since the ratio is always 4, this is a direct variation!

Step 2: Write the Equation

Problem: Find the constant of variation and write the equation.

The constant of variation (k) is 4.
To write the equation, plug our k into the core format y = kx.
The equation is y = 4x.

Error to Avoid: Dividing x by y instead of y by x!

When finding the constant of variation, the formula is strictly k = y / x. A very common mistake is flipping this and doing x / y. Always put the y-value on top!

Section 4 • 5 Minutes Direct Instruction

Objective 2: Solving for Missing Values

Core Method: Find 'k', then use y=kx
Example Problem Statement:

The value of y varies directly with x, and y = 24 when x = 4. Find y when x = 7.

Step 1: Find the Constant of Variation (k)

Problem: y = 24 when x = 4.

First, use the known pair to find k.
k = y / x
k = 24 / 4
k = 6

Step 2: Write Equation & Solve for New Value

Problem: Find y when x = 7.

• Substitute our new k (6) into the equation: y = 6x
• Now, plug in the new x value (7): y = 6(7)
• Multiply: 6 * 7 = 42
The value for y is 42.

Two-Step Process
1. Find k
k = 24 / 4 = 6

2. Use new equation
y = 6x

y = 6(7)
Final Answer (y): 42

Error to Avoid: Forgetting to Write the Equation First!

Don't try to guess the answer directly in one step. Always write down the new equation (y = kx) with your discovered 'k' before you plug in the second number.

Section 6

Key Takeaways & Save / Turn In

1. The Constant (k)

The constant of variation is found by dividing y by x. k = y / x. This must be true for every point in the relation!

2. The Equation

All direct variations can be written as y = kx. If it has a "+ b" at the end (like y = 2x + 1), it is NOT a direct variation.

3. Origin Point

Because the equation is y = kx, if x is 0, y is 0. The graph of a direct variation always passes through the origin (0,0).

4. Proportional

Direct variation means two things are proportional. If you double the input, you automatically double the output.
